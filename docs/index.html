Pretrain a Tiny BERT, Then Fine-Tune Four Heads

How it was built

Part 1: Text, Entities and Packed Inputs

Everything the encoder will read. A word-level tokenizer on TinyStories, a corpus with people and places planted into it so the labeled tasks later have ground truth by construction (section 15.7.1.5's Mrs Green and New York), a vocabulary with BERT's special tokens, and the [CLS] A [SEP] B [SEP] packing of eq 15.73.

Part 2: The Bidirectional Encoder

A transformer encoder in tinygrad, built from the book's pseudocode in section 15.5.4: the three embeddings of Fig 15.34, multi-head self-attention (eqs 15.54-15.56) with a padding mask and no causal mask, the post-norm encoder block, and an encoder that returns every layer so the scalar-mix probe can look inside it.

Part 3: The Two Pretraining Objectives

The self-supervised losses from sections 15.7.1.3 and 15.7.1.4: the masking procedure behind the negative log pseudo-likelihood of eq 15.71, the masked language model's softmax with tied embeddings (eq 15.72), next-sentence pairs in the format of eq 15.73, and the classifier on the [CLS] token.

Part 4: Pretraining

A hand-written Adam and one TinyJit loop that minimizes the masked and next-sentence losses together, then the measurements that tell you whether anything was learned: cloze accuracy against a baseline, the next-sentence score, fill-in-the-blank on the book's own example, and how much of the model's accuracy comes from the words to the right of the blank.

Part 5: Looking Inside: An ELMo-Style Scalar Mix

Section 15.7.1.1 says each task can learn its own weights over the layers of a language model, and that syntactic tasks should favour lower layers while semantic tasks favour higher ones. You build that learned mix as a probe on the frozen encoder, and test the claim with a closed-class tagging task and a word sense task.

Part 6: The Fine-Tuning Machinery

Section 15.7.1.5 fine-tunes by "simply adding one or more new output heads to the final hidden layer". This part builds the small MLP head, a checkpoint you can restore, and one compiled training loop that runs the same head from a pretrained encoder, from a frozen encoder or from random weights, so the comparison is fair and does not pay for recompiling.

Part 7: Four Heads

The tasks of Fig 15.35 on held-out names, places and words: sentence classification from [CLS], pair classification from [CLS], BIO tagging with a constrained decoder and entity-level F1, and question answering with start and end heads. Each is run from random weights and from the pretrained encoder, on the same few labels. The last step runs the whole study.
